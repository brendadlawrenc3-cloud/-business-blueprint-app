# The ALIGN Framework™
### A Strategic Framework for AI Business Integration

> **AI should not determine your business strategy. Your business strategy should determine where AI creates value.**

**Central question for the CEO:** *Where can AI strengthen our ability to execute our strategy, improve performance, and build a more valuable enterprise?*

**Value chain:** Business Strategy → Business Processes → AI Opportunities → Execution → Business Outcomes

The objective isn't AI adoption. It is better business performance, with AI as one of the means.

---

## 1. The five executive decisions

| | Phase | Decision | Question | Deliverable |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | Assess Business Priorities | **WHY** | What strategic outcome justifies AI investment? | AI Strategic Alignment Map |
| **L** | Locate Value Opportunities | **WHERE** | Which processes, constraints, or capabilities offer the greatest opportunity? | AI Value Opportunity Map |
| **I** | Identify AI Readiness | **WHEN** | Is the organization ready, and what must happen first? | AI Integration Readiness Assessment |
| **G** | Govern Integration & Execution | **HOW** | How will AI be integrated into operations, people, governance, and execution? | AI Integration Business Case & Roadmap |
| **N** | Navigate Performance & Scale | **WHAT** | What measurable value is AI creating, and how will we sustain it? | AI Business Performance Scorecard |

ALIGN repeats. The trigger events that restart the cycle are listed in §9.

## 2. A — Assess Business Priorities (WHY)

AI initiatives come from business priorities, not from what the technology can do.

**Executive questions**
1. What are our three most important business objectives?
2. What is preventing us from achieving them?
3. Where are we experiencing performance gaps?
4. Which activities consume disproportionate time or resources?
5. What capabilities must improve for the business to scale?
6. *(added)* Where could AI change how competitors, customers, or new entrants compete in our market?

**Alignment Map fields:** priority · objective · metric · baseline · current · target · executive owner · constraint · constraint type.

### The "Is this an AI problem?" diagnostic *(added)*
The framework says leaders must recognize when a problem doesn't need AI. This diagnostic makes that a required step. Each constraint is classified by its root cause:

| Root constraint | AI fit | Guidance |
|---|---|---|
| Capacity | AI lever | Absorb repetitive cognitive work. Plan where freed capacity goes. |
| Cycle time | AI lever | Compress research, drafting, analysis. Map the full cycle first. |
| Consistency | AI lever | Encode standards once the standard exists. |
| Insight | AI lever | Surface patterns and forecasts; only as good as the data. |
| Knowledge access | AI lever | Make institutional knowledge self-serve; needs captured knowledge. |
| Process design | Partial | Redesign first. AI on a broken process accelerates the breakage. |
| Skills / talent | Partial | AI augments, but hiring, training, or role design may be the fix. |
| Accountability | **Not AI** | Fix ownership, cadence, consequences. |
| Leadership alignment | **Not AI** | Resolve at leadership-team level. |
| Strategy clarity | **Not AI** | Clarify strategy before any AI investment. |

**Exit criteria:** 3–5 priorities with baseline/current/target, a named owner for each, and every constraint classified.

## 3. L — Locate Value Opportunities (WHERE)

Look at how the business creates and delivers value, not only at where employees *could* use AI.

| Function | Potential opportunity | Business outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Sales | Lead qualification, forecasting | Higher conversion |
| Marketing | Segmentation, campaign analysis | Lower acquisition cost |
| Operations | Workflow optimization, capacity planning | Lower operating cost |
| Finance | Cash-flow forecasting, analysis | Financial visibility |
| Customer service | Faster inquiry resolution | Higher satisfaction |
| Human resources | Workforce planning, knowledge management | Organizational capacity |
| *(added)* Product / service | AI-enhanced or new AI-enabled offerings | New revenue, differentiation |

These are possible uses, not recommendations.

### The Business Value Test
1. What business problem are we solving?
2. What financial or operational impact does this problem create?
3. Why is AI appropriate rather than conventional process improvement or automation?
4. What measurable result would justify the investment?

### Value hypothesis *(added)*
Every opportunity carries numbers, so ideas can be compared on the same basis:

- **Annual value** if the hypothesis holds
- **Confidence** (0–100%) that it will be realized
- **One-time cost** (design, integration, data prep, training)
- **Annual run cost** (licences, usage, support, *and human oversight time*)

Derived: risk-adjusted value = value × confidence; net annual value = risk-adjusted − run cost; payback = one-time ÷ net annual value.

### Levels of integration
| Level | Purpose | Example | Measured by |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 · Optimize | Improve existing operations | Faster proposals | Cost, cycle time, productivity |
| 2 · Transform | Redesign how work gets done | AI-enabled sales delivery process | Capacity, margin, customer outcomes |
| 3 · Reinvent | New capabilities or revenue models | Sellable AI-enabled advisory service | New revenue, differentiation, enterprise value |

The levels don't have to happen in order. A healthy portfolio funds several Optimize moves and deliberately explores at least one Transform or Reinvent bet.

## 4. I — Identify AI Readiness (WHEN)

| Dimension | Question | Critical? | 1 / 3 / 5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strategic clarity | Do we know what outcome we want? | — | None / named / measurable with owner |
| Process maturity | Is the process documented and understood? | — | Person-dependent / partial / stable & measured |
| Data readiness | Is data accurate, accessible, usable? | **Yes** | Missing / scattered / governed |
| Leadership readiness | Is there an accountable sponsor? | — | None / interested / accountable & resourced |
| Workforce readiness | Do employees have skills and support? | — | Resistant / curious / trained & involved |
| Governance readiness | Can we manage privacy, security, reliability, compliance? | **Yes** | None / informal / policy & controls |

**Classification rules (scores are never simply averaged):**
- **Defer or Redesign:** strategic clarity ≤ 2, or the business value is negligible.
- **Prepare Before Implementing:** any critical dimension ≤ 2, any dimension at 1 or 2, or an average below 3.5.
- **Ready to Pilot:** every dimension ≥ 3 and an average ≥ 3.5.

Readiness gaps become **funded work items**, not footnotes.

## 5. G — Govern Integration & Execution (HOW)

AI integration is an organizational change initiative, not a technology project.

**Five questions:** Process · People · Technology (tool-agnostic) · Governance · Performance.

### Integration business case
| Component | Example |
|---|---|
| Business objective | Improve sales conversion |
| Current challenge | Proposals take five business days |
| AI-enabled capability | Assist with proposal research and drafting |
| Target outcome | Two-day turnaround |
| Process owner | VP of Sales |
| Process change *(added)* | Research and first draft move to an AI-assisted analyst step |
| People change *(added)* | Analysts trained; partners redeploy time to client meetings |
| Oversight model *(added)* | Human decides / **human approves** / human monitors |
| Human oversight | Sales leadership reviews all proposals |
| Baseline captured *(added)* | Yes, before the pilot starts |
| Success metrics | Turnaround, quality, conversion |
| Pilot period | 60–90 days |
| Scale criteria *(added)* | Turnaround ≤ 2.5 days and win rate ≥ 26% at day 90 |
| Stop criteria *(added)* | Not below 4 days at day 60, or any factual error reaching a client |

### Mandatory risk gates
Each gate is rated Pass / Conditional / Fail. **Any Fail stops the opportunity, whatever its score.** No pilot starts while a gate is still unassessed.

1. Data privacy · 2. Security · 3. Legal, regulatory & IP · 4. Reliability & accuracy · 5. Customer & reputational trust *(added)* · 6. Vendor dependency & exit *(added)*

## 6. N — Navigate Performance & Scale (WHAT)

| Dimension | Example measures |
|---|---|
| Financial | Revenue, gross margin, EBITDA, cost savings |
| Operational | Cycle time, productivity, error rates, capacity |
| Customer | Retention, satisfaction, response times |
| Organizational | Capacity, adoption, decision quality |

Progress = share of the baseline→target gap closed.

### Value realization chain *(added)*
**Hours freed → redeployed to → business outcome moved.**
Time saved is not automatically value created. If an initiative reports hours saved but cannot name where they went and what outcome moved, it is reported as *activity*, not *value*.

### Scale / Iterate / Stop
- **Scale:** ≥ 80% average gap closure *and* efficiency linked to an outcome
- **Iterate:** 25–80%
- **Stop:** < 25% (apply the pre-agreed stop criteria)

## 7. The Executive Decision Matrix

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---|
| Strategic alignment | 25% |
| Expected business value | 25% |
| Process & data readiness | 20% |
| Implementation feasibility | 15% |
| Organizational adoption readiness | 15% |

Score 1–5 → weighted → normalized to 0–100.

**Decision rules (applied in order):**
1. Any risk gate fails → **Do Not Pursue**
2. Alignment + value average < 2.5, or risk-adjusted value ≤ run cost → **Do Not Pursue**
3. Outcome undefined → **Do Not Pursue** (defer / redesign)
4. High value (≥ 3.5) but not ready → **Build Readiness**
5. Gates not yet assessed → no pilot until they are
6. High value + Ready to Pilot + confidence ≥ 60% + score ≥ 70 → **Invest Now**
7. Otherwise → **Pilot & Validate** cheaply

The thresholds are a starting point. Calibrate them against real CEO cases (see §10).

## 8. Gap analysis: what the draft needed, given the audience and goals

**Audience:** CEOs and leadership teams of small and mid-market companies, often in peer-advisory settings. Many have no CTO or data team.
**Goals:** a repeatable decision method, sold as an assessment, a workshop, and an advisory engagement.

The gaps below are listed by impact. Items marked ✅ are now part of the framework and the app.

| # | Gap | Why it matters for this audience | Resolution |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **No economics model** ✅ | CEOs think in payback and margin. "What result would justify the investment" needs numbers, or every idea looks equally good. | Value hypothesis: value × confidence, one-time and run cost, payback. Run cost includes human-oversight time. |
| 2 | **"Is this an AI problem?" had no tool** ✅ | The draft's most important distinction relied on judgment alone. | Constraint diagnostic with ten root-cause types and an AI-fit classification. |
| 3 | **No baseline requirement** ✅ | Without a pre-pilot baseline, results can't be attributed, so N can't work. | Baseline is a required field in G. Without it, the business case is incomplete. |
| 4 | **Vague "expand only if…"** ✅ | Pilots with no exit plan drift on for months. | Scale and stop criteria are agreed *before* the pilot. N gives a verdict. |
| 5 | **Time-saved trap had no mechanism** ✅ | The "300 hours" insight is a key differentiator but wasn't operational. | Value realization chain. Unlinked efficiency is flagged as activity, not value. |
| 6 | **People & workforce impact underweighted** ✅ (partial) | Fear of job loss is the most common reason adoption stalls. CEOs need a position on it before rollout. | People-change field, workforce-readiness dimension, and a missing-people-change flag. **Still to build:** a workforce communication template and a "capacity redeployment, not reduction" policy option. |
| 7 | **Risk gates too generic** ✅ | "Privacy, security, legal" misses the risks small firms hit most. | Six named gates, including customer trust and vendor dependency/exit. |
| 8 | **No defensive/competitive lens** ✅ (question) | Reinvent covers upside but not the threat, e.g. AI eroding billable-hour pricing in professional services. | Added A-question #6. **Still to build:** a short "AI exposure" check: which revenue streams could a competitor using AI undercut? |
| 9 | **No current-state inventory ("shadow AI")** ⚠️ | Most companies already have staff using AI tools without policy. This is the most common live risk. | **Recommended addition to A:** a 15-minute inventory of current AI use, plus a minimum acceptable-use policy as the first governance deliverable. |
| 10 | **No portfolio / change-capacity view** ✅ (partial) | A $5M company can absorb only 1–3 concurrent changes. | Portfolio counts by decision and level on the Overview. **Recommended:** a limit on concurrent pilots, set by company size. |
| 11 | **No governance cadence or decision rights** ⚠️ | The framework names deliverables but not who decides and when. | Opportunities can be sent into the app's Decision Flow. **Recommended:** a quarterly AI portfolio review in the leadership meeting rhythm, and a RACI (CEO approves Invest Now; process owner runs the pilot; a governance lead clears gates). |
| 12 | **Build / buy / partner choice** ⚠️ | Staying tool-agnostic is right, but G eventually needs a way to choose. | **Recommended:** tool-agnostic selection criteria applied *after* the business case: data handling, integration, total cost, exit terms, vendor viability. |
| 13 | **Sustain phase thin** ⚠️ | AI performance drifts, usage costs creep, and owners move on. | **Recommended:** ownership after the pilot, quarterly cost review, accuracy re-sampling. Reassessment triggers are included (§9). |
| 14 | **Size calibration** ⚠️ | A $3M firm and a $150M firm need different depth. | **Recommended:** "ALIGN Essentials" (one workshop, top 3 opportunities) and "ALIGN Enterprise" (full diagnostic) using the same logic. |
| 15 | **IP / naming risk** ⚠️ | "ALIGN" is widely used in business frameworks. | Run a trademark search before using ™/®. Consider a distinctive qualifier (e.g. "ALIGN AI Integration Method"). |
| 16 | **No validation protocol** ⚠️ | The draft rightly says to validate with real CEOs before formalizing. | See §10. |

**Positioning recommendation:** Go with **Option A (AI Strategic Integration Framework)** as the foundation. The Transform and Reinvent levels already leave room to grow into enterprise transformation without making every engagement a transformation program. It is also an easier first purchase for a CEO.

## 9. Reassessment triggers
- Annual or quarterly strategic-plan refresh
- A strategic priority achieved, dropped, or materially changed
- A competitor or new entrant uses AI to change pricing, speed, or service
- A significant shift in AI capability or cost
- A pilot reaches its scale-or-stop point
- A new regulation, client contract term, or security incident

---

## 10. Validation protocol (before formalizing the IP)
1. Run ALIGN with **5–8 CEOs** across 2–3 industries and revenue bands.
2. For each, record: time to complete; whether the top-ranked opportunity matched the CEO's gut call (and why or why not); which questions confused them; and which decision rules they overrode.
3. Calibrate the thresholds (3.5 high value, 70 Invest Now, 60% confidence) against those overrides.
4. Follow up at 90 days: did Invest Now pilots hit their scale criteria? Did any "Do Not Pursue" call turn out wrong?
5. Only then fix the name, weights, and packaging.

## Using the app
In the Blueprint app, open **AI Integration** in the sidebar (or go to `#align`). Every phase, the readiness rules, the risk gates, and the Decision Matrix work interactively, starting from a worked example: a $5M professional services firm growing to $8M. The decision logic is in `src/utils/alignScoring.ts`, and the framework content is in `src/data/alignFramework.ts`. Edits are saved in the browser.
